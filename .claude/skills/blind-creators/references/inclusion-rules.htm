Inclusion Rules and Editorial Rulings

These are the standing decisions for the four directories. They govern every evaluation. Apply them as written; if a case does not fit, say so in chat and mark the candidate pending rather than bending a rule.

The default-No rule

Positive verification is required for every fact that an entry rests on: that the person is blind or has low vision, that the work exists at a working public link, and that the person created or led it. Not finding anything that disqualifies someone is never grounds to include them.

Three outcomes

  • Include — every criterion below is met on evidence you opened yourself.
  • Pending — evidence is insufficient (a claim you could not confirm, a link that would not open, a role that is not clearly the person’s own). Record what is missing in chat so the next session can pick it up.
  • Excluded — a confirmed disqualifying fact (sighted, work abandoned before 2020 for developers, co-authored only, and so on). Record the fact.

Blind Authors

Every one of these must be true:

  • The author was blind or had low vision when the book was written.
  • The author is the sole author. “Collaboration” means named individual co-authorship; a book with two named authors does not qualify, and a person with only co-authored books is not listed.
  • First published in 2000 or later.
  • Available today in a digital form — electronic text or audio — through a working link that you opened.

Blind Developers

Every one of these must be true:

  • The developer is blind or has low vision.
  • They created or led the development of the app or tool. Team software counts when the person was a creator or a development lead, but everyone listed has at least one app of their own.
  • The app is reachable today through a working link to where it can be obtained and used.
  • Actively developed or maintained in 2020 or later.

Ruling: an app that cannot be verified at a web location you opened must not count toward inclusion and must not be mentioned in the document at all. The developer may still be included on the strength of a different, verified app.

Ruling: Community Access organization products may be attributed to Jeff Bishop when no other developer is identifiable.

Ruling: Michael Curran (NVDA), Glen Gordon (JAWS), and Saqib Shaikh (Seeing AI) are unaffected by the collaboration definition; each created or leads the product.

Blind Presenters

Every one of these must be true:

  • The presenter is blind or has low vision.
  • The work is contemporary, from 2010 onward.
  • The work shows sustained, notable leadership rather than a one-off clip — at least one of: a keynote or featured address at a recognized event; an established, regularly published series; a body of at least three published works; or professional standing such as a broadcasting role, festival selection, or major recognition.
  • The presenter holds the role in their own right; everyone listed has at least one series or body of presentations created on their own.
  • The presenter is credited with at least three public presentations of their own. Collaborative presentations can count in addition, but the three that qualify must be theirs alone.

Blind Creators

A person appears here when they appear in at least one of the three source directories. The category tests are the ones above. The file is generated by scripts/buildCreators.py; never edit it by hand.

Entry-level rulings that apply everywhere

  • Up to three works per entry, newest first (most significant first when dates are unknown), each with a working link and a one-line description.
  • Profiles: website, GitHub, LinkedIn, App Store developer page, Amazon, Goodreads, Wikipedia, podcast — whatever exists and was opened — sorted alphabetically by label. A person with no profile page simply has no Profiles line.
  • Try to gather a full Profiles set and check for up to three qualifying works for every new addition.
  • No editorial commentary in the documents: no “link unverified,” no “could not find,” no hedging. All of that goes in the chat reply.
  • English-language scope: the work and the sources must be followable in English. A developer whose store page is in English qualifies even if their website is not.
  • Versioning: a new entry bumps the minor version (v2.20.1 to v2.21.0); a correction or enhancement bumps the patch (v2.20.1 to v2.20.2). The version lives in the YAML front matter and is what the release tag is taken from.
  • Counts: the subtitle (“35 Creators of Apps While Blind or Low Vision”) and the intro sentence (“This directory lists 35 developers”) change with every addition or removal. Match the noun to the count.

Who is not a candidate

  • A person whose contribution is a feature or patch inside someone else’s product, or a patched fork of another person’s library, without a product of their own.

  • The person who forwarded an email or moderates a list.

  • A tester, beta user, or “user group” helper for someone else’s product.

  • An organization. Only a named individual is listed.

  • A person whose only evidence of blindness is that they build for blind users.
