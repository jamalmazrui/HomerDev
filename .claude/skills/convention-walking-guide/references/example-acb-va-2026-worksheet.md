## Check Common Tasks Floor by Floor

For each task, record the exact starting point, destination, supported directions, arrival cue, and return access. Group by task in the attendee guide; a room-name index can cross-link to these tasks.

- Arrive by public transit: service/station/stop, operating day, pickup side, transfer or final pedestrian connection, and actual hotel entrance.
- Arrive by taxi, rideshare, or shared shuttle: exact property, loading entrance, curb/drop-off, and reception connection. Keep these services distinct.
- Check in: entrance to hotel reception; distinguish hotel check-in from convention packet pickup.
- Reach a guest room: relevant elevator bank, floor name/number, discharge direction, room-number ranges, useful odd/even patterns, corridor decisions, and door identification.
- Get ice, soda, or snacks: machine/market on each relevant floor, exact door or alcove, payment/access, and route back to elevators or room corridor.
- Use elevators: hall call buttons, which side of the doors, car panel location, floor-button row/column order, lobby/convention labels, braille/tactile labels, door-open/close/alarm controls, spoken announcements, key-reader use, and differences among cars. Describe actual controls rather than assuming a standard layout.
- Use stairs: floor-specific door locations, ordinary access versus emergency-only use, landing labels, reentry, and step-free alternatives. Do not infer an everyday stair route from an evacuation symbol.
- Find restrooms: public facilities on the lobby, convention, and other useful floors, entrances and labels, and route back to the event doorway.
- Get a seated meal, collect food, or buy drinks: restaurant counter versus table service, pickup point, hours, hotel delivery handoff, and actual pedestrian access to nearby options.
- Attend general sessions, workshops/committees, or exhibits: room/floor, usable entrance for the setup, foyer connection, and schedule.
- Use gym, pool, laundry, or business center: actual entrance, level, operating hours, and key access.
- Use dog relief: assigned area, surface, waste bin, level changes, and reentry.

Promote only supported task details into attendee directions. Use a short operational contact where useful; keep general travel teaching in the optional appendix.

## Walkthrough Record

Use one record per route; publish the supported directions after review.

- Route name and exact origin/destination door, property, and floor.
- Source URL or drawing, revision, and date checked.
- Starting position and facing direction.
- Actions, intersections, fixed landmarks, and arrival cue.
- Level changes and continuous step-free alternative.
- Return route, key requirements, opening hours, and after-hours reentry.
- Transport boarding/drop-off point and public entrance connection, if applicable.
- Confirmation cue, overshoot cue, and practical restart point.
- Event room configuration and temporary obstructions.
- Checker/date, cane-user and guide-dog-user feedback, and approved wording.
- Update contact, observed change, and next review.

Keep provider-published facts, provider-issued changes, and traveler observations distinguishable. A walkthrough records conditions at a particular time. Obtain permission before sharing names, photos, or personal locations.

## Editorial Rules

Lead attendee text with venue and event orientation. Put general discovery and mobility suggestions in an optional appendix. Use numbered steps only for supported routes, with an identifiable starting position. Keep open research tasks here. A map’s top edge does not establish north, and room adjacency does not establish a corridor route. Avoid invented sensory cues, exact distances, handling commands, or assumptions about which kind of help a traveler wants. Keep ordinary access separate from emergency egress.

Use one H1, logical H2/H3 headings, linked contents, short descriptive links, and simple lists. Attendee and worksheet Markdown use UTF-8 BOM with CRLF; SKILL.md uses UTF-8 without BOM.
