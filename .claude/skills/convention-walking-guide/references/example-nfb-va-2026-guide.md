## Take Your Guide Dog to Relief

### Conference-Center Relief Area

Start at the **outside door across from Salons D and E**, facing outward.

1. Open the outside door and go out.
2. Proceed left to the grass.

The agenda places trash cans near the outside doors. Confirm the return door and any key requirement.

### Sleeping-Room Relief Area—Three Steps Down

Start **entering the hotel lobby**, facing into it.

1. Continue straight, then take the first left into the first-floor guest-room hallway.
2. Go to the end, where there is a door to a stairway.
3. Turn right and leave through the outside exit.
4. Outside, turn right and go down **three steps**.
5. Turn right to the grass-and-dirt area.

A trash can is near the outside door. For a step-free option or return-door access, contact convention operations. These directions are from the convention agenda.

## Appendix: Optional Route-Learning Notes

For travelers who want a refresher or a framework for exploring a new venue:

- Choose the form of directions that works for you: room relationships, left/right turns, compass bearings, a route demonstration, or a combination.
- Build a personal map around useful anchors such as your elevator landing, reception, and the convention foyer. Add destinations in whatever order suits your plans.
- A route note can record the starting position, turns, arrival cue, return door, and a place to restart. Distinguish permanent features from temporary crowds, furniture, sounds, and smells.
- For a route you want to explore, useful questions are: What confirms my position? What marks an overshoot? Where can I resume from a known point? Check return access separately.
- For an outing, connect the transport stop to the actual entrance and plan the return pickup. Keep useful directions and contact numbers offline.

These optional ideas draw on Thomas Bickford’s [The Care and Feeding of the Long White Cane](https://nfb.org/sites/default/files/images/nfb/publications/books/cfcane/canetc.htm), especially its discussion of environmental clues and mental maps, and the supplied structured-discovery and travel-data documents. Bickford emphasizes individual differences in how travelers use landmarks and spatial information.

## Appendix: Free Mobile Tools

For signs, menus, outdoor orientation, and remote visual assistance. Stop out of pedestrian flow for camera tasks. GPS tools are for outdoor orientation; camera descriptions can be mistaken. Camera tools need camera permission; calls and many AI features need internet. GPS needs location permission and works poorly indoors.

### Seeing AI: iPhone and Android

Free. Read room signs or menus. Guide dog user Deborah Armstrong describes sign reading in her [2024 account](https://applevis.com/blog/staying-oriented-using-ai-powered-apps-tool-further-independence); blind commenters report aiming difficulties. [Official app site](https://www.seeingai.com/). Test offline functions rather than assuming all modes work without data.

### Google Lookout: Android

Free, Android 6 or later; Google account required. Text mode reads signs; Explore identifies objects. Low-vision user Veronica Lewis describes reading posted schedules in her [review, updated 2023](https://veroniiiica.com/google-lookout-app-for-low-vision/). Descriptions can miss details. [Google help and download](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/9031274). Seeing AI serves a similar sign-reading purpose on iPhone.

### Be My Eyes: iPhone and Android

Free volunteer video calls and Be My AI; account required. Ask a volunteer to read a sign or identify a visible door while you are stopped. Blind users report both helpful calls and uneven volunteer skill in this [community discussion](https://server.applevis.com/forum/assistive-technology/problems-bemyeyes-volunteers). AI can misread scenes. [Downloads for both platforms](https://support.bemyeyes.com/hc/en-us/articles/360005528717-Download-and-sign-up-for-Be-My-Eyes).

### Apple Maps and Google Maps

Free: Apple Maps on iPhone; Google Maps on iPhone and Android. Use them for outdoor walking/transit planning, not ballroom doors. Blind users' older [Apple Maps discussion](https://www.applevis.com/forum/ios-ipados/using-maps-app-vs-blind-square) and [Google Maps reports](https://www.applevis.com/apps/ios/navigation/google-maps) describe differing results; comments span older app versions. Routes may end at a driveway. [Apple Maps](https://www.apple.com/maps/); [Google navigation help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/3273406). Downloaded maps do not guarantee offline walking/transit guidance.

### Indigo Nav: iPhone and Android

Free outdoor GPS with spoken route information and waypoint recording. [APH's page](https://www.aph.org/product/indigo/) links both stores and tutorials. [User reports](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/indigo-nav/id945756779?platform=ipad&see-all=reviews) include blind-user praise under earlier app names and later location errors. Try it on a familiar outdoor route; it cannot establish the right hotel door or a clear path through traffic.

### VoiceVista: iPhone

Free download with sponsorship purchases listed. Preview streets and save an outdoor entrance marker. Blind users discuss benefits and excessive announcements in the [community review](https://www.applevis.com/apps/ios/navigation/voicevista). [Download](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/voicevista/id6450388413). Indigo Nav and Google Maps offer outdoor routes on Android, but are not equivalent spatial-audio tools. GPS markers are approximate.

Built-in Magnifier is another free iPhone option, but LiDAR-dependent door and people detection need supported hardware. Check [Apple's requirements](https://support.apple.com/en-sg/guide/iphone/iph35c335575/ios); do not assume every iPhone supports them. Seeing AI and Lookout are alternatives for camera sign reading.
