# Convention Walking Guide

## Define audience and outputs

Decide from the request whether the guide serves one traveler or all attendees. Default convention-program requests to all attendees; ask only if a material ambiguity remains. Keep personal devices, interests, and itineraries out of public guides. Deliver a concise attendee guide and a separate organizer worksheet with distinct filenames. Preserve requested guide filenames. Keep private correspondents, uploaded filenames, research inventories, source conflicts, and detailed uncertainty in the worksheet. Refer to sources by kind in attendee directions.

## Establish the venue before routing

Identify organization, year, dates, hotel name, full street address, and overflow properties from current organizer sources. Inventory uploads before searching. Detect mixed events, similarly named hotels, stale URL paths, renamed rooms, and headings reused over previous-year content. Never transfer routes between properties. Preserve the user's requested filenames.

Read [research-and-validation.md](references/research-and-validation.md) for source strategy, floor-plan analysis, validation prompts, and acceptance criteria. Read [guide-template.md](references/guide-template.md) when drafting. Use [source-examples.md](references/source-examples.md) as methodological examples, never as current venue directions.

## Gather and reconcile evidence

Seek current convention agenda/program, organizer orientation text, Braille Forum and Braille Monitor articles, affiliate newsletters, hotel floor plans, accessibility maps, and blind-authored walkthroughs. Open sources rather than relying on search snippets. Download original PDFs/DOCX when possible. Visually inspect floor plans at readable resolution as well as extracting text. Track source date, floor, property identity, and contradictory statements. Search current public sources for each material gap before labeling it unknown. Record remaining gaps and conflicting evidence once in the organizer worksheet. Check live page content rather than assuming a preliminary agenda is unavailable because the full program is not posted.

Keep route evidence status in the organizer worksheet. In attendee text, use a brief source attribution where it helps distinguish published directions from map relationships; avoid a repeated tag taxonomy or an inventory of absent information. Reserve Walked for an actual documented walkthrough, not remote map inspection. Do not call a map-derived route verified merely because the map is official. Never invent distances, door counts, compass orientation, sensory cues, or a clear path through an unlabeled connection. Distinguish sighted map adjacency from a usable pedestrian connection. Do not interpret fire-egress arrows as ordinary accessible travel routes.

## Organize by traveler task

Make tasks the primary structure: arrive from transit or by rideshare; check in at the hotel; reach the assigned room; collect convention registration; use elevators/stairs; find a public restroom; get ice, soda, meals or takeaway; attend general sessions and workshops/committees; visit exhibits; use the gym; take a dog to relief. Distinguish hotel reception from convention registration, and seated dining from counter service, pickup, markets, and delivery. Use clear action headings and a linked contents list. Preserve the mental map within task explanations or offer a compact location index as an appendix. Avoid duplicating directions across sorts.

Research each common task even when the existing program is organized by room. Record exact elevator call/car panel locations and button order, floor labels, key-reader use, room-number patterns, machine alcoves, public restrooms by floor, stair reentry, and amenity access only from evidence or an actual walkthrough. Keep open collection tasks in the organizer worksheet, not empty attendee sections or absence reports. A hotel's feature list establishes availability, not a route. For each published task, check whether its directions actually reach the destination from the stated start rather than merely naming it. Use a concise operational contact for a supported service when appropriate, without implying a route was established.

## Build a usable mental map

Introduce a small number of stable hubs: entrance, front desk, elevator landing, convention foyer. Explain room groups, partitions, floor changes, and guest-versus-conference elevators. Translate page-relative observations into traveler-relative turns only with a stated position and facing direction. If that orientation is unknown, retain a compact mental map and put the missing connection in the worksheet. Do not generate long tentative turn sequences or artificial starting positions that require a helper to place every attendee. Use independently findable landmarks. Use cardinal directions when justified by a north arrow, a verified entrance bearing, or reliable geospatial evidence; never equate the top of a page with north.

For each route specify start and facing direction, destination, numbered actions, decision landmarks, stop/arrival cue, level changes, evidence status, and return/reentry conditions where known. Give independently checked reverse directions when supported; do not silently reverse a route involving one-way doors, ramps, or asymmetric elevator landings. Supply a recovery action at ambiguous decisions. Keep a quick attendee route section ahead of technical evidence discussion.

Put general structured-discovery, first-arrival practice, confirmation/overshoot questions, and recovery teaching in an optional appendix. Lead the main guide with location and event orientation for experienced blind travelers. Omit sections for unavailable facts and sentences reporting failed searches; place actionable research tasks in the worksheet. Let the traveler choose how to learn. Treat sounds, smells, crowds, and movable furniture as changing clues unless observed and qualified. Never transfer another city's floors, street bearings, sensory landmarks, or keycard procedures.

Read [learning-and-route-data.md](references/learning-and-route-data.md) when adding learning resources, transit handoffs, app-coverage claims, or route-maintenance fields. Distinguish provider static information, provider current changes, and traveler observations independently of route confidence. Connect boarding/drop-off to the actual public entrance; add dated updates and fallbacks where supported. Offer offline route notes and a correction channel without sharing private location or contacting others unless authorized.

## Cover both travel methods

Write for competent blind travelers with individual preferences. Include useful wall/corridor/intersection information for cane users and destination/door cues for guide dog handlers without prescribing handling commands. Identify relief surface, waste bins, step-free access, operating hours, and return-door key requirements when known. Treat guide dog relief as a convention-specific assignment, not every grassy patch. Check guest-room number patterns, elevator labels/announcements and banks, transport loading locations, and nearby food routes where relevant. Do not claim food is walkable from distance alone. If adding nearby outings, state the specific nonvisual access, actual entrance, current arrangements, complete travel/return time, and booking contact. Ordinary narration, natural listening value, audio description, touch permission, and wheelchair access are different claims; verify each and avoid blanket accessibility ratings. When the main hotel sells out, cover the overflow property, its own relief arrangements, interhotel service, and drop-off-to-registration route; distinguish unknown boarding points from known shuttle schedules. Never transfer airport/local departure intervals to the interhotel shuttle. Describe ordinary exits separately from emergency egress. Offer assistance and orientation as options, not requirements.

## Add the mobile-tools appendix

Research current free built-in and third-party tools. Require firsthand blind- or low-vision-user experience in addition to official availability/platform/price evidence. Distinguish firsthand reviews, developer claims, community comments, and editorial recommendations. Include specific convention uses and limitations, dates, direct links, account/data/network requirements, and hardware restrictions. Inspect actual redirect destinations, app IDs, publisher identities, and review dates; reject unrelated/repurposed links. Check whether reviews belong to a predecessor product. Separate genuinely free functionality from trials, optional sponsorships, and paid tiers. Do not describe free download as free service. Do not assume indoor mapping, beacons, or navigation coverage at the venue. State hardware requirements generally for public guides; tailor to a specific device only for a named traveler. Balance platforms: pair iPhone-only functions with an Android option or explain that no equivalent was verified. Accept firsthand low-vision-user reviews with clear attribution as well as blind-user reviews. Prefer short entries with platform/cost, concrete use, main limit, and review/source links. Record review dates and separate older experience from current official feature evidence.

Keep camera OCR/AI description distinct from obstacle detection and safe street-crossing decisions. Encourage familiar-setting practice, stopping out of pedestrian flow for camera tasks, and keeping environmental audio available. Prefer a short useful selection over an unverified app catalog.

## Deliver and validate

Target roughly ninth-grade language and an attendee guide under about 1,500 words including its app appendix unless the user requests more. State common cautions once, with route-specific hazards at the relevant heading. Describe supported facts directly; attach material source qualifications without repeated disclaimers. Do not manufacture complete directions to avoid acknowledging a gap. Keep detailed methods and worksheets separate. Use screen reader friendly Markdown: one H1, H2 sections, H3 route headings, linked contents, short descriptive links, numbered route steps, and simple lists rather than tables unless requested. Include sources and an as-of date. Keep research provenance out of every step but attach source labels to route blocks. Record what was inspected and research limitations in the organizer worksheet. Keep attendee text focused on useful supported orientation.

Check every turn against the source and starting orientation. Check all routes for missing connectors and all links/anchors for consistency. Validate dates, weekdays, time zone, and nearby daylight-saving changes using an actual calendar/time-zone source. Do not assume the clock change occurs on the convention Sunday. Keep attendee Markdown and worksheets in the user's requested encoding/line endings; retain SKILL.md as UTF-8 without BOM for frontmatter compatibility. Separate attendee directions from the organizer worksheet. Where authorized, obtain an independent dry run of the skill on raw inputs and correct failures before saving. Do not contact venues, email organizers, or publish externally unless the user authorizes those actions. Package requested deliverables, validate archive contents, and save them using the environment's persistence workflow.

## In a Homer project

Added to the Homer Development Kit on 19 October 2026 from the skill developed with ChatGPT on two 2026 Virginia state conventions. Everything above applies; these rules add the Homer conventions.

- **Tasks first, always.** Every main section is a task a traveler sets out to do, never a place. A sort by floor, wing or room is allowed only as an appendix that links back to the tasks; never repeat the directions there. [guide-template.md](references/guide-template.md) opens with this rule.
- **Worked examples.** The two Virginia cases are in references: [the ACB of Virginia guide](references/example-acb-va-2026-guide.md) and [its worksheet](references/example-acb-va-2026-worksheet.md), and [the NFB of Virginia guide](references/example-nfb-va-2026-guide.md) and [its worksheet](references/example-nfb-va-2026-worksheet.md). Read them for structure, tone and length. Never reuse their turns, rooms, phone numbers or arrangements for another event or year.
- **Names and encoding.** Name files `<Organization>_<State>_Convention_<Year>_Convention_Walking_Guide.md` and `..._Organizer_Worksheet.md`. Write them in the Homer encoding, UTF-8 with a byte order mark and CRLF, with one H1, and make each .htm with Pandoc beside it.
- **Check with the kit.** Run the homer-docs skill's checkDocs on the guide before handing it over: one H1, headings in order, no bare links, and the reading grade, which should be near ninth grade.
- **The agenda as a database.** When the agenda itself is wanted as something to search, plan and share, the convention-database skill builds it in DbDo; use the same room names in both, so a session found there is the place this guide leads to.
- **A guide is shared with others.** Name no private individual; name organizations, venues and public contact points only. Keep each fact's grounding in the worksheet, as this skill already requires.
